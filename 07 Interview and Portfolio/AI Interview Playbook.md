# AI Interview Playbook

Interviewers usually test: conceptual fluency, problem framing, role craft, evidence, tradeoffs, collaboration, risk judgment, and learning speed.

## Scenario answer frame

1. Clarify user, decision, scale, consequence, baseline, data, and constraints.
2. Define acceptable outcome and evaluation before choosing technology.
3. Offer a simple baseline and 2–3 viable options.
4. Sketch system/workflow and ownership boundaries.
5. Describe representative tests, slices, failure modes, and thresholds.
6. Cover security, privacy, safety, accessibility, rights, and escalation proportionately.
7. Explain rollout, observation, rollback, cost, and next experiment.
8. State assumptions and what would change the recommendation.

## Common questions

- When should this not use AI?
- Prompting, RAG, fine-tuning, or training—how do you choose?
- How would you evaluate an LLM feature?
- What would make you stop launch?
- How do you handle model/provider change?
- How do you prevent prompt injection and unsafe tools?
- How do you measure value beyond usage?
- Tell me about a failure or disagreement.
- How do you keep current without chasing every release?

## Good signals

- precise assumptions and calibrated language;
- decomposition before tool selection;
- metrics tied to consequences;
- respect for adjacent experts and escalation;
- recognition of system and human workflow;
- alternatives and reversibility;
- evidence from your own decisions.

## Questions for the employer

1. What outcome does this role own in the first six months?
2. Which title aliases or responsibilities are bundled into it?
3. Who owns data, evaluation, production, risk acceptance, and incidents?
4. What evidence is required to launch or change a model?
5. How representative and accessible are user research and evaluation?
6. Which tool/vendor constraints are fixed, and which are open?
7. How is on-call/review burden distributed?
8. What distinguishes strong performance at this level?

> [!warning] Interview integrity
> Follow interview rules. Do not secretly use an AI assistant where prohibited. If allowed, disclose its role and remain able to defend every answer and artifact.

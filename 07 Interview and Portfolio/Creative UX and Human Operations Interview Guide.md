# Creative, UX, and Human Operations Interview Guide

## UX and conversation

Prepare to map user mental models, trust calibration, uncertainty/refusal/recovery states, accessibility, human handoff, research with affected non-users, memory controls, and longitudinal evaluation. Critique an interface whose fluent output encourages overreliance.

## Creative and media

Present a brief-to-final workflow, creative direction, iteration/selection, human contribution, consistency, tool choice, provenance, input and likeness rights, disclosure, accessibility, quality control, and brand safety. Your portfolio should show taste and production judgment—not a gallery of unedited generations.

## Annotation, feedback, and knowledge operations

Expect task design, guideline writing, sampling, calibration, agreement, adjudication, ambiguity, worker well-being, vendor operations, ontology/taxonomy decisions, source authority, and quality monitoring. Be able to explain why disagreement can be data.

## Support, user operations, and trust and safety

Separate ordinary product questions, reliability incidents, account/security problems, abuse, and imminent safety risks. For a synthetic case, show triage, evidence minimization, policy/version use, severity, uncertainty, authorized action, handoff, user communication, appeal or recovery, QA, and the product/control feedback loop. Balance service speed with decision accuracy, affected-user outcomes, recurrence, and worker wellbeing.

## Training/enablement

Define learners and outcomes, teach mental models and failure handling, design retrieval checks and suggested projects, measure transfer to work, handle outdated materials, and separate training completion from authorization.

## Portfolio review pattern

Brief/problem → users/affected groups → constraints → alternatives → iterations → evaluation → failure and repair → rights/risk/accessibility → final artifact → learning.

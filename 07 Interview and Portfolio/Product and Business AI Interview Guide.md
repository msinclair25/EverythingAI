# Product and Business AI Interview Guide

## Core questions

- Identify and prioritize AI opportunities for a business function.
- Decide whether to build, buy, partner, or not use AI.
- Define a proof of value and release criteria.
- Measure adoption, quality, review burden, and ROI.
- Handle a model change that breaks a workflow.
- Explain an AI system to executives, users, risk teams, and engineers.
- Design governance that does not block low-risk experimentation.
- Recover from an AI feature that users distrust.

## Product case structure

User/workflow → evidence and baseline → outcome → AI suitability → risks/constraints → options → evaluation → UX/control → rollout/adoption → economics → decision.

## Business/consulting case structure

Objective → value pools → process/data readiness → portfolio and sequencing → operating model → workforce/change → governance → financial scenarios → measures and decision gates.

## Customer-facing case structure

Discovery → confirmed requirements → gaps/assumptions → architecture → representative proof of value → security/data/cost → adoption plan → honest limitations → next step.

## Go-to-market and partnership case structure

Segment/account/partner thesis → stakeholder and workflow evidence → differentiated value → proof and no-claim zones → technical/risk feasibility → economics and incentives → decision/negotiation process → launch or mutual action plan → adoption/support handoff → measured learning, renewal, or exit.

## Executive AI leadership case structure

Enterprise objective → portfolio thesis and non-AI alternatives → operating model and decision rights → talent/data/platform readiness → investment and risk gates → adoption/change → value and incident reporting → stop/scale/retire decisions. State explicitly which specialists retain legal, security, model-risk, architecture, and business authority.

Do not answer with a vendor list. Show how evidence leads to a choice.

# Foundations Final Check

Respond in 3–6 sentences per scenario. Name the decision, evidence, and at least one limitation.

## Scenarios

1. A team reports 96% accuracy for a hiring-screen classifier. What do you ask before recommending use?
2. A policy assistant cites five documents, but users find incorrect answers. Decompose the evaluation.
3. A customer wants to upload a confidential archive to a consumer AI product. What must be checked?
4. An agent can read email and issue refunds. Propose the minimum permission and approval design.
5. A prompt revision improves 40 curated examples. What evidence is still missing?
6. A model's benchmark score rises while production complaints rise. Give plausible explanations and next steps.
7. A vector index returns an obsolete policy above the current one. Which architecture and governance controls help?
8. A generated image campaign is visually strong. What U.S. rights/provenance review remains?
9. A call-center copilot cuts handling time but increases after-call corrections. How should value be measured?
10. A model-based judge scores a new system higher. How do you validate the judge and conclusion?
11. Input distribution changed last week. Should the model be disabled? Explain the decision process.
12. A vendor promises “bias-free AI.” Rewrite the claim into assessable questions.
13. A chatbot refuses legitimate disability-accommodation requests. Which disciplines and controls are needed?
14. An automation retries a payment tool after a timeout. What failure can occur and how is it controlled?
15. A small local model is slightly less accurate than a hosted model. What other dimensions may change the choice?

## Progression

A strong result names most elements without copying, prioritizes the highest-consequence issue, and avoids claims beyond evidence. Revisit corresponding foundation modules for any scenario where you cannot explain **why** an element matters.

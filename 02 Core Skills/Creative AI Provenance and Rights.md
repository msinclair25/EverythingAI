# Creative AI Provenance and Rights

This is issue-spotting education, not legal advice. Creative teams need a record connecting authorized input, human contribution, tool terms, output review, and publication.

## Production record

Capture brief, creator, date, tool/model/version, account/license tier, source assets and permissions, prompts/settings where appropriate, generated candidates, human selection/arrangement/edits, likeness/voice consent, trademark/brand review, factual/safety checks, content credentials/metadata, final approver, and distribution channels.

## U.S. copyright orientation

The U.S. Copyright Office's January 2025 Part 2 report says purely AI-generated material is not copyrightable; sufficient human-authored expression is required and assessed case by case. AI assistance does not disqualify an otherwise human-authored work. Prompting alone does not automatically supply authorship, while human-authored inputs, creative selection/arrangement, and modifications may matter. Registration requires appropriate disclosure of AI-generated material.

Separate questions about output copyrightability from training-data legality, licenses, contract terms, trademark, publicity/likeness, privacy, defamation, and platform policy.

## Safer practice

Use authorized references, avoid requests to imitate living artists or protected brand identity without approval, obtain informed voice/likeness consent, preserve edit history, label synthetic content where required/appropriate, and establish takedown/correction response.

## Quiz

1. Is AI assistance itself a bar to U.S. copyright? 2. Does prompting alone guarantee authorship? 3. Why preserve edit history? 4. Name three non-copyright rights issues.

> [!success]- Answers
> 1. No. 2. No; sufficient human-authored expression is case-specific. 3. It documents human contribution, provenance, review, and reproducibility. 4. Contract/license, trademark, publicity/likeness, privacy, defamation, or disclosure law/policy.

## Flashcards

Human authorship:: Human-created expressive contribution required for U.S. copyright protection.

Content provenance:: Evidence about an artifact's source, generation, edits, and custody.

Digital replica:: A digitally created or manipulated realistic representation of a person's voice or appearance; rights and rules extend beyond copyright.

Source: [U.S. Copyright Office AI initiative](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/), including Parts 1–3; verified 2026-08-12.

# U.S. AI Law and Policy Orientation

> [!danger] Orientation only
> Laws, executive policy, agency authority, litigation, and state effective dates change. Use this note to spot issues, then verify primary authority with qualified counsel or policy owners.

## The U.S. pattern at the cutoff

U.S. AI governance combines existing generally applicable law, sector rules, targeted federal AI statutes, executive-branch policy for federal agencies, voluntary frameworks/standards, enforcement, contracts, and rapidly changing state/local laws. Do not infer that “no AI-specific rule” means no legal obligation.

## Current federal policy landmarks

- [Executive Order 14179](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/removing-barriers-to-american-leadership-in-artificial-intelligence/) (2025) directed an AI Action Plan and revoked EO 14110.
- [America's AI Action Plan](https://www.whitehouse.gov/srv/htdocs/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf) (July 2025) sets administration priorities; it is a policy plan, not a private right of action.
- [Executive Order 14365](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/) (December 2025) directs executive-branch actions concerning state AI laws and development of a federal legislative recommendation. The order does not itself enact Congress's proposed nationwide framework or erase every state AI law.
- [OMB M-25-21 and M-25-22](https://www.whitehouse.gov/omb/information-resources/guidance/memoranda/) govern covered federal agency AI use and acquisition.
- [OMB M-26-04](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/M-26-04-Increasing-Public-Trust-in-Artificial-Intelligence-Through-Unbiased-AI-Principles-1.pdf) addresses “Unbiased AI Principles” for federal LLM use/procurement.
- [Executive Order 14409](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/) (June 2026) directs federal AI/cybersecurity actions, including a classified frontier-model benchmarking process and a voluntary developer framework; it expressly says that framework does not authorize mandatory model licensing or preclearance.
- [NSPM-11](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/national-security-presidential-memorandum-nspm-11/) (June 2026) governs AI in the national-security enterprise, calls for adoption, assurance, accountability, updated governance and evaluation, and rescinds/replaces NSM-25. It is not the general rule for all private or civilian AI use.
- The [March 2026 White House National Policy Framework legislative recommendations](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/03.20.26-National-Policy-Framework-for-Artificial-Intelligence-Legislative-Recommendations.pdf) are recommendations, not enacted legislation.

## Issue map

| Use | Authorities/issues to investigate |
|---|---|
| Employment | Title VII, ADA, ADEA and other employment/civil-rights rules; EEOC guidance; state/local automated-employment rules |
| Consumer claims | FTC Act and sector consumer-protection authority; substantiation, deception, unfairness |
| Credit/housing | ECOA, FCRA, FHA and regulator guidance as applicable |
| Health | FDA oversight for relevant medical products, HIPAA/privacy and sector/state rules |
| Education | FERPA, disability/civil-rights duties, state/student rules |
| Government | procurement, records, privacy, civil rights, accessibility, security, and OMB/agency policy |
| Creative/media | copyright, contract/license, trademark, publicity/likeness, privacy, defamation, disclosure/deepfake rules |
| Children | COPPA and other federal/state child privacy/safety rules |
| Biometrics | state biometric privacy and sector/context rules |

## State and local change

Use the [NCSL AI Legislation Database](https://www.ncsl.org/financial-services/artificial-intelligence-legislation-database) for discovery; it was updated monthly and last showed July 1, 2026 at verification. Then read the enacted bill, amendments, regulator rules, effective date, definitions, exemptions, preemption, and enforcement from the jurisdiction's official source.

## Analysis method

1. actor and role: developer, deployer, employer, vendor, government, user;
2. jurisdiction and effective date;
3. system/use definition and exclusions;
4. data and affected population;
5. decision and consequence;
6. notice, consent, documentation, testing, human review, appeal, and reporting duties;
7. contract allocation vs nondelegable obligations;
8. evidence, retention, incident, and enforcement exposure.

## Quiz

1. Why can existing law apply without saying “AI”? 2. Are White House legislative recommendations law? 3. Why is an NCSL entry not final authority? 4. Does a vendor contract automatically transfer all obligations?

> [!success]- Answers
> 1. Rules govern conduct and consequences such as discrimination, deception, privacy, safety, and rights regardless of technology. 2. No. 3. It is a discovery index; verify enacted text, amendments, effective date, and rules. 4. No; contracts allocate some responsibilities but applicable duties and accountability may remain.

## Flashcards

Generally applicable law:: Law governing conduct or outcomes across technologies, which can apply to AI use.

Effective date:: The date a legal requirement begins to operate, which may differ from enactment.

Developer vs deployer:: The actor building/supplying a system versus the actor using it in a context; definitions vary by law.

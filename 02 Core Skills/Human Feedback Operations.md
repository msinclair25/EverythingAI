# Human Feedback Operations

Human feedback operationalizes preferences, corrections, demonstrations, critiques, safety judgments, and domain expertise for training and evaluation.

## Feedback types

- absolute rating under a rubric;
- pairwise or listwise preference;
- demonstration of desired behavior;
- critique/error taxonomy;
- edit from draft to accepted output;
- policy/safety classification;
- tool trajectory or task-success review.

Each answers a different question. Preference is not truth, and a pleasing answer may be less correct.

## Program design

Define objective and decision use; sample tasks; write rubrics and anchors; recruit appropriate expertise; calibrate; blind/randomize where appropriate; capture rationale without unnecessary sensitive data; measure agreement and systematic differences; adjudicate; protect worker well-being; audit incentives; version data and policy; connect feedback to downstream evaluation.

Watch position bias, verbosity/style preference, identity cues, cultural assumptions, reviewer fatigue, policy drift, contamination, and vendor labor conditions.

## Quiz

1. Why is preference not factual correctness? 2. Why randomize pair order? 3. What is calibration? 4. Why preserve rubric version?

> [!success]- Answers
> 1. Humans can prefer fluent, agreeable, or familiar output that is wrong. 2. To detect/reduce position bias. 3. Practice and comparison that align reviewers with task definitions and reveal ambiguity. 4. Label meaning changes when policy or anchors change.

## Flashcards

Pairwise preference:: A judgment selecting the better of two outputs under defined criteria.

Reviewer calibration:: Aligning task interpretation through guidance, examples, practice, and feedback.

Policy drift:: Change in how a feedback rule is defined or applied over time.

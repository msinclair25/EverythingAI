# AI Video Producer

> [!abstract] Mission
> Produce finished, truthful-to-context, rights-cleared video by combining story, direction, controlled generation, live-action/assets, editing, sound, continuity, provenance, and delivery.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    Brief["Brief + audience + truth conditions"] --> Pre["Script, boards, shots, releases"]
    Pre --> Produce["Generate/shoot/source"]
    Produce --> Edit["Picture, continuity, VFX, sound"]
    Edit --> Review["Claims, likeness, disclosure, accessibility"]
    Review --> Deliver["Masters + captions + provenance"]
```

## Responsibilities

- Develop brief, treatment, script, storyboard/animatic, shot list, visual grammar, schedule, budget, and rights plan.
- Direct generated and captured performances, cameras/motion, continuity, characters/objects, lighting, and environments.
- Manage source assets, prompts/references/settings, versions, approvals, likeness/voice/property/music rights, and provenance.
- Edit story and rhythm; composite/VFX, color, titles, mix, captions, audio description where needed, QC, and encode deliverables.
- Verify factual/real-world claims and label realistic synthetic or altered media under applicable platform/client rules.
- Create contingency plans for generation failure, inconsistency, vendor restrictions, and takedown/incident response.

## Boundaries

- “AI video creator” may be social generation only; producer implies budget, schedule, team, rights, postproduction, and delivery accountability.
- Editor shapes existing footage; producer owns broader project execution, though small teams combine them.
- [[Generative AI Creative Director]] owns overarching concept/system across media.

## Levels

| Level | Scope | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Finished 15–30 second piece | Coherent edit, clean sound/captions, process evidence |
| Producer | Multi-shot story and production package | Continuity, rights/provenance, on-spec delivery |
| Senior | Brand/narrative/high-risk/multi-vendor production | Team direction, clearance, budget, failure recovery |
| Lead / executive | Slate, pipeline, standards, client/brand accountability | Repeatable quality and responsible synthetic-media practice |

## Prerequisites and competencies

- Story structure, script, shot language, directing, editing, continuity, composition/light, frame rate/shutter basics, codecs/resolution/color, audio, titles, captions, project/media management.
- Generative controls: text/image/video-to-video patterns, references, camera/motion prompting, temporal artifacts, consistency, lip-sync limits, upscaling/interpolation tradeoffs.
- Rights/ethics: releases for face/voice/performance, music/footage/license terms, deceptive synthetic media, current platform disclosure, U.S. copyright human authorship.
- A generation prompt is not a release, license, fact check, or complete production plan.

## Study sequence

1. Edit a strong 30-second story from licensed stock before generating anything.
2. Create treatment, boards, animatic, shot list, and production/rights matrix.
3. Generate shots using image-to-video and controlled references; log iterations and continuity problems.
4. Composite/edit/color/mix/caption; replace weak generation rather than hiding it with speed.
5. Run factual, likeness/voice, representation, provenance, disclosure, and accessibility review.
6. Deliver platform-specific masters, project archive, source/license/release package, and case study.

## Tools

- **Free-first:** DaVinci Resolve, Blender, Kdenlive, OBS, Audacity, GIMP/Krita, FFmpeg, subtitle tools, licensed public-domain/Creative Commons assets with verification.
- **Paid:** Premiere/After Effects/Audition/Firefly, Final Cut, Runway and other video model services, Frame.io, stock/music libraries, professional caption/QC services.
- Credit pricing and generation limits are volatile; budget by approved finished shot, not raw generation count alone.

## Knowledge check

1. Why create an animatic before expensive generation?
2. Name four continuity dimensions.
3. What YouTube content generally requires synthetic-content disclosure?
4. Why are captions not merely a transcript dump?
5. What belongs in a synthetic-performer release/record?

> [!success]- Answers
> 1. It tests story, timing, coverage, and shot need cheaply. 2. Character identity/costume, object, space/screen direction, light/color, motion, time, eyeline—any four. 3. Meaningfully altered/generated content that appears realistic in its specified categories, including making a real person appear to do something they did not. 4. They need timing, speaker identification, sound cues, readability, accuracy, and line discipline. 5. Identity/authorized party, exact voice/likeness/performance, purpose/media/territory/duration, model/storage/reuse, edits, compensation, disclosure, revocation/takedown and signatures as applicable; obtain legal review.

## Flashcards

Animatic:: Timed storyboard with provisional sound used to validate story and shot plan.
Screen direction:: Consistent spatial movement/gaze orientation that helps viewers understand geography.
Picture lock:: Approved edit stage after which image timing should not change without controlled reopening.
Model release:: Permission for use of a person’s appearance in specified contexts; synthetic reuse needs explicit scope.
Synthetic-media disclosure:: Notice that meaningful realistic content was generated or altered, required in certain contexts by platforms or law/policy.
Delivery master:: High-quality approved source file from which platform versions are encoded.

## Interview preparation

- Break down a 30-second brief into coverage, generation pattern, rights, schedule, budget, and fallback.
- Diagnose a continuity-broken sequence and choose regenerate, composite, cutaway, or rewrite.
- Explain disclosure for a realistic fictional event or real person.
- Bring treatment, script, boards/animatic, shot/prompt log, releases/licenses, edit timeline, captions, masters, and provenance.

## Suggested projects

- **Beginner:** 20-second non-photorealistic sequence with animatic, six shots, sound, captions, and source log.
- **Intermediate:** 60–90 second hybrid live/stock/generated short with continuity bible, compositing, original/cleared audio, disclosure analysis, and platform masters.
- **Advanced:** branded mini-documentary or narrative with real-world claims: research ledger, releases, synthetic policy, edit/fact review, accessibility, C2PA/provenance where supported, incident/takedown plan, and audience test.

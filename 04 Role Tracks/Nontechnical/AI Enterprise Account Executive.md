# AI Enterprise Account Executive

> [!info] October 13, 2026 update
> Use [[US Career and Governance Update — October 2026]] for current U.S. evidence and a role-family work-product exercise. Pair it with [[Model and Agent Lifecycle Management]] to explain how a technical change affects users, risk, cost, and communication. Older profile claims retain their original verification dates.

> [!abstract] Mission
> Lead a truthful, evidence-based enterprise buying process that connects an AI capability to an important customer outcome, earns executive and technical confidence, and reaches a sound commercial decision.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    Account["Account and industry hypothesis"] --> Discover["Multi-stakeholder discovery"]
    Discover --> Qualify["Value, fit, risk, authority, process"]
    Qualify --> Prove["Demo or proof with mutual criteria"]
    Prove --> Decide["Commercial and risk decision"]
    Decide --> Close["Negotiate and close"]
    Close --> Adopt["Accurate success handoff and expansion evidence"]
```

## Responsibilities

- Build a territory and account plan from customer priorities, industry context, likely workflows, competitive position, and credible value hypotheses.
- Develop executive, business, technical, security, legal, procurement, finance, and operational relationships across a complex account.
- Qualify the outcome, baseline, urgency, sponsor, users, data, risks, implementation capacity, buying process, economics, and decision criteria.
- Orchestrate [[AI Sales Engineer]], [[AI Solutions Architect]], product, security, legal, finance, and leadership through evaluation and contracting.
- Maintain an evidence-based pipeline, next step, mutual action plan, forecast, commercial strategy, and clean handoff to [[AI Customer Success Manager]].
- Protect trust by distinguishing current capability, configuration, partner work, roadmap, and unsupported requests; do not turn aspiration into a promise.

## Adjacent-role boundaries

- The account executive owns account strategy, commercial process, executive alignment, negotiation, closure, and forecast; [[AI Sales Engineer]] owns technical discovery, demo, and proof evidence.
- [[AI Solutions Architect]] owns deeper target architecture and feasibility; the executive does not approve security or architecture on that specialist’s behalf.
- [[AI Customer Success Manager]] owns post-sale adoption and outcome realization; the seller remains accountable for an accurate handoff.
- [[AI Product Manager]] decides product direction; sales supplies market evidence but cannot commit a roadmap item.
- Legal, security, privacy, finance, and deal-desk owners approve their respective terms and risks. Escalating a blocker is not authority to waive it.

## Levels

| Level | Scope | Evidence of readiness |
|---|---|---|
| Sales development / commercial feeder | Prospecting, research, qualification support | High-quality account hypotheses and disciplined discovery notes |
| Account executive | Bounded enterprise territory or segment | Runs a complete, accurate buying process and closes with a viable handoff |
| Senior / strategic AE | Large, regulated, or multi-division accounts | Aligns executives, evaluators, risk owners, and economics through long cycles |
| Account director / sales leader | Portfolio strategy, coaching, forecast, and operating system | Improves win quality, forecast integrity, adoption, and ethical selling behavior |

## Prerequisites and competencies

- Enterprise discovery, consultative selling, executive communication, negotiation, account mapping, pipeline judgment, forecasting, and business-case reasoning.
- Practical literacy in AI capabilities and limits, APIs/data/cloud, evaluation, security/privacy, governance, change management, and deployment economics.
- Ability to calculate value from an explicit baseline and adoption assumptions, not from generic productivity slogans.
- Written precision: decision criteria, commitments, dependencies, exclusions, owners, dates, and evidence must survive the handoff.
- Industry or workflow expertise is often decisive in regulated or specialized accounts; bring authorized experts rather than bluffing.

## Study sequence

1. Learn one AI product and one target industry from official product, security, data-handling, and regulatory sources.
2. Build an account thesis, stakeholder map, discovery guide, and value tree for one enterprise workflow.
3. Practice qualification that covers outcome, baseline, sponsor, users, data, risk, technical fit, decision process, economics, and change capacity.
4. Partner with a sales engineer on a demo or proof-of-value plan with representative cases and mutual success/stop criteria.
5. Simulate security, privacy, procurement, legal, pricing, and negotiation stages using an assumptions and approval ledger.
6. Create a forecastable mutual action plan and an adoption-ready handoff; review which claims and commitments the evidence supports.

## Tools

- **Free-first:** Obsidian, spreadsheets, Mermaid, public company filings, official product/security documentation, LinkedIn’s free surface, video calls, and a simple CRM template.
- **Paid:** enterprise CRM, sales intelligence and engagement, conversation intelligence, configure-price-quote, proposal/e-signature, security-questionnaire, forecasting, and customer-success platforms.
- Customer notes may contain confidential plans and personal data. Use approved systems, access controls, and retention rules; never paste restricted account data into an unapproved assistant.

## Knowledge check

1. What makes an AI account qualified beyond customer interest?
2. Why is an executive sponsor insufficient on their own?
3. Who owns the technical proof decision?
4. What turns a value estimate into a defensible business case?
5. How should a seller handle an unapproved roadmap request?
6. What must an implementation handoff preserve?

> [!success]- Answers
> 1. A consequential outcome, baseline, users, sponsor, technical/data fit, risk path, decision process, economics, timeline, change capacity, and mutual next action. 2. Deployment also needs users, technical owners, risk/procurement approvers, operational ownership, budget, and implementation capacity. 3. The buying team owns its decision; the AE orchestrates, while the sales engineer/architect supplies honest technical evidence. 4. Explicit baseline, affected volume, benefit mechanism, adoption, cost, risk, time horizon, sensitivity analysis, and owner. 5. Label it as a gap/request, document the use case, route it to product, and make no commitment without authorized written approval. 6. Outcome, scope, stakeholders, architecture assumptions, data/risk constraints, evaluation evidence, commercial commitments, exclusions, dates, and owners.

## Flashcards

What is an account hypothesis?:: A testable view of a customer priority, workflow, value mechanism, stakeholders, and likely constraints.
What is enterprise qualification?:: Evidence that value, fit, authority, process, risk, economics, and implementation capacity can support a real decision.
What is a mutual action plan?:: A shared sequence of evidence, approvals, owners, dependencies, and dates leading to a decision and launch.
What is forecast integrity?:: Representing deal stage, probability, timing, evidence, and risk honestly enough for the business to plan.
What is a commercial red line?:: A term or condition that only an authorized legal, security, finance, or deal owner may approve or change.
What is expectation debt?:: Future delivery and trust damage created by vague, inflated, or unauthorized pre-sale commitments.
What is a viable handoff?:: Transfer of complete evidence, commitments, context, ownership, and constraints to the adoption team.

## Interview preparation

- Present a named-account plan with three testable hypotheses, a stakeholder map, land-and-expand logic, risks, and next evidence—not a list of company facts.
- Run discovery before pitching. Show how the current workflow, baseline, decision process, and implementation capacity change your recommendation.
- Defend a business case with sensitivity analysis and explain when the deal should be narrowed, delayed, or disqualified.
- Role-play a request to promise a roadmap item, security property, or business outcome you cannot authorize.
- Bring a mutual action plan, forecast rationale, proof plan, negotiation assumptions, and implementation handoff.

## Suggested projects

- **Beginner:** Produce an account brief for a public U.S. company with workflow hypothesis, stakeholder map, ten discovery questions, and explicit unknowns.
- **Intermediate:** Simulate a complete enterprise opportunity: discovery, value case, proof plan, risk/procurement path, mutual action plan, forecast updates, and no-surprises handoff.
- **Advanced:** Create an industry territory system with segmentation, account scoring, partner motion, value/risk models, coaching rubric, forecast inspection, and a post-sale quality review.

# August 2026 Research Brief

## What the evidence supports

AI is a broad capability layer changing existing occupations and creating specializations, but there is no official census of “every AI job.” BLS tracks many relevant workers inside established Standard Occupational Classification categories, so novel title counts and salaries should not be inferred from adjacent occupations.

The [2026 Stanford AI Index](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report) reports that organizational AI adoption reached 88% in its underlying survey evidence, U.S. private AI investment reached $285.9 billion in 2025, and 1,953 newly funded U.S. AI companies were recorded. These figures describe investment/adoption evidence, not guaranteed labor demand for every title. Verified 2026-08-12.

BLS 2024–2034 projections show faster-than-average growth in several adjacent occupations:

| BLS occupation | Projected growth | Why it matters | Do not infer |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Data scientists | 34% | data, modeling, insight, and ML work | that every “AI data scientist” shares one scope/pay |
| Information security analysts | 29% | security demand adjacent to AI security | a count of AI security engineers |
| Computer and information research scientists | 20% (19.7% table value) | research, algorithms, robotics, computing | a count of AI research scientists |
| Software developers | 16% (15.8% table value) | software systems, including AI applications | a count of AI engineers |
| Computer and information systems managers | 15% | technology planning/leadership including AI | a count of AI transformation leaders |
| Management analysts | 9% | workflow/strategy consulting adjacent to AI transformation | a count of AI strategy consultants |

Sources: [BLS Data Scientists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm), [BLS occupational projections table](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm), [BLS CIS Managers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/computer-and-information-systems-managers.htm), [BLS Management Analysts](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/management-analysts.htm), verified 2026-08-12. Values are projections, not guarantees.

## Technical direction

Agentic systems, rigorous evaluation, security, retrieval/knowledge quality, multimodal generation, and inference efficiency are important cross-role themes. NIST launched an AI Agent Standards Initiative in February 2026; its further outputs were still emerging. NIST also published AI 800-3 on statistical models for evaluation and an ARIA pilot report, while automated benchmark best-practice material was in draft/public-comment status. This supports teaching evaluation science and agent controls without pretending the ecosystem is settled.

## U.S. policy direction

The current federal executive-policy context includes [EO 14179](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/removing-barriers-to-american-leadership-in-artificial-intelligence/), the [July 2025 AI Action Plan](https://www.whitehouse.gov/srv/htdocs/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf), [EO 14365](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/) concerning a national AI-policy framework and state-law challenges, [OMB M-25-21/M-25-22](https://www.whitehouse.gov/omb/information-resources/guidance/memoranda/) for covered federal agency use/acquisition, [M-26-04](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/M-26-04-Increasing-Public-Trust-in-Artificial-Intelligence-Through-Unbiased-AI-Principles-1.pdf) regarding “Unbiased AI Principles” for federal LLM procurement/use, [EO 14409](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/) on advanced-AI innovation and cybersecurity, and [NSPM-11](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/national-security-presidential-memorandum-nspm-11/) for the national-security enterprise. These instruments have different actors and scopes: an executive order or memorandum is not automatically a private-sector statute, and EO 14365's requested legislative framework is not itself enacted legislation. The separate [March 2026 White House national-policy document](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/03.20.26-National-Policy-Framework-for-Artificial-Intelligence-Legislative-Recommendations.pdf) also contains legislative recommendations rather than enacted law.

Existing law and enforcement remain relevant to conduct involving discrimination, disability/accessibility, consumer deception/unfairness, privacy, intellectual property, safety, and sector decisions. State legislation remains active and must be verified jurisdiction by jurisdiction.

## Curriculum implications

1. Teach role functions and aliases, not a brittle title list.
2. Give every role evaluation, data, security/privacy/safety, economics, and human-workflow literacy.
3. Treat prompt/context skill as cross-functional; a standalone “prompt engineer” title may appear but is not the only home for that work.
4. Teach free/open/local paths plus enterprise products and their tradeoffs.
5. Keep creative, governance, human-feedback, customer, and transformation work equal citizens—not footnotes to engineering.
6. Re-check policy, tools, models, prices, and title demand on a recurring schedule.

# US Career and Governance Update — October 2026

> [!abstract] Read career evidence at the right scale
> National occupational statistics help with context. They do not determine the salary, education requirements, or hiring probability of every job called “AI engineer.” Governance frameworks help structure work; they are not automatic legal compliance.

## Updated labor anchor

The [BLS Data Scientists page](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm), checked October 13, reports **May 2025 median annual pay of $120,230**, **35% projected employment growth for 2025–2035** (rounded), and about **24,800 openings per year** over that period. Openings include replacement needs; they are not all newly created positions. These figures apply to the BLS occupation, not all 63 roles in this vault.

Use this window instead of the older 2024–2034 reference when discussing current projections. Preserve [[August 2026 Research Brief]] as a historical note. Do not relabel May 2025 wages as “2026 AI salaries.”

### A career-research method you can defend

1. Pick a functional role from [[AI Career Atlas]], then collect a small, dated sample of real U.S. job postings yourself.
2. Record employer, location, remote restrictions, level, duties, required versus preferred skills, and posted compensation basis where available.
3. Map duties to this vault's work products rather than relying on job title alone.
4. Separate base salary, bonus, equity, and contract hourly pay. Do not average unlike measures.
5. Report sample size, selection method, duplicates, and uncertainty. A small convenience sample is not a national labor estimate.

No new employer-posting sample was collected for this release. The exercise is a suggested method, not fabricated hiring evidence.

## Governance anchors checked this release

[NIST's AI RMF page](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) still presents AI RMF 1.0 and revision work. Its GenAI profile is a companion resource; revision activity and concept notes are not a newly finalized replacement standard. The framework is voluntary unless another applicable authority or contract makes particular requirements binding.

[California's CPPA rulemaking page](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html) records final regulations covering cybersecurity audits, risk assessments, and automated decisionmaking technology, approved September 22, 2025 and effective January 1, 2026. **Effective date is not necessarily every compliance deadline.** Applicability, phased obligations, exceptions, and current litigation require the operative text and qualified legal review.

This is educational U.S. context, not legal advice or a complete federal/state survey. The older [[US Policy Source Register]] remains a discovery map; its entire executive-order and agency table was not reverified in this release.

## Nontechnical and hybrid work products

| Role family | Current practice exercise | Good evidence | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product/program | Plan a retiring AI-service migration | User impact, dependencies, owners, acceptance criteria | Treating an API swap as zero user impact |
| UX/research | Design degraded-service and approval states | Comprehension study plan and accessible alternatives | Hiding uncertainty or making approval meaningless |
| Governance/risk | Translate a policy into a testable control | Applicability analysis, control owner, evidence schedule | Calling a framework a certification |
| Legal/procurement | Compare vendor service boundaries | Data terms, export rights, change notice, review questions | Assuming app subscriptions include API rights |
| Customer success/support | Prepare for an agent-memory incident | Scope-aware script, escalation path, recovery criteria | Promising no impact before investigation |
| Sales/solutions | Explain what the system can and cannot do | Bounded demo, assumptions, measured limitations | Presenting a synthetic benchmark as a customer result |
| Learning/change | Teach safe use after a feature migration | Before/after workflow, knowledge check, feedback plan | Training only on buttons instead of decisions |
| Content/creative | Reassess a retired media API | Rights/provenance checklist and alternative workflow | Confusing an API sunset with all consumer availability |

These original exercises extend the current layer to nontechnical tracks without asserting new market demand for every title.

## Quiz and answers

1. **Is $120,230 the expected offer for a new AI engineer?** No; it is a national occupation median with a specific wage year.
2. **Are all projected openings newly created jobs?** No; replacement openings are included.
3. **Does a rule's effective date settle every compliance date?** No; read phased requirements and scope.
4. **Does an NIST-aligned checklist establish legal compliance?** No; applicability and evidence still need review.

## Flashcards

Occupation versus job title::A statistical category differs from an employer's label for a particular bundle of duties.
Wage reference year::The period measured by a pay statistic, distinct from when you read it.
Replacement openings::Vacancies from people leaving or changing occupations, not only employment expansion.
Effective versus compliance date::When a rule takes legal effect can differ from when a particular obligation must be satisfied.
Framework versus law::A voluntary organizing tool is not automatically a binding legal requirement.
Convenience sample::A sample selected for accessibility that may not represent the broader population.

## Suggested project

Create a two-page role brief for one U.S. role: a properly labeled BLS context, a small transparent job-posting sample, three recurring work products, and a learning plan tied to those products. For a governance path, add a source-status table distinguishing final rules, guidance, drafts, and voluntary frameworks.
